# Nuvora — Brand Brief

*The name is decided. This brief now documents the finalized brand, not a naming decision — kept as the reference for tone, visuals, and positioning.*

## What Nuvora actually is

Nuvora is an all-in-one "Life OS" for students — one app that replaces the five or six apps someone would otherwise juggle to organize their tasks, their studying, their focus time, and their headspace. It's built by Haneen Turkieh, a 19-year-old solo developer and CS student in the Computer Science Apprenticeship Program (CAP) at An-Najah National University, Nablus, Palestine, and it's bilingual (English/Arabic) from the ground up.

The pitch in one line: **a calm, slightly poetic productivity app that turns discipline into something you actually want to do.**

## The pillars (what's actually inside)

- **Tasks & goals** — everyday to-dos, recurring tasks, milestones, a day planner.
- **Focus & the Forest** — a pomodoro-style focus timer where every completed session plants a tree in your personal Forest. Trees can die if you break focus. There are also shared "Rooms" — synced focus sessions with friends, so studying together isn't just a video call, it's a shared timer and a shared tree.
- **Habits & mood** — streak tracking plus daily mood logging.
- **Lumi** — an AI companion woven through the whole app. It remembers things across conversations, can log your mood or check your habit streaks for you, nudges you about deadlines, and has a warm, slightly poetic personality (ask it who built it and it answers like it means it).
- **Exam Assistant** — upload your notes or files, get an AI-generated practice exam (multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, mixed) with a real difficulty rubric and a real countdown timer. It's a personal exam simulator, not just a flashcard generator.
- **Launchpad** — a CV builder plus internship/project tracker, for the "what comes after graduation" part of student life.
- **Analytics & gamification** — XP, levels, streaks, growth charts. Discipline becomes visibly, satisfyingly a game.
- **Premium** — a subscription (monthly/semester/annual, priced in NIS) that unlocks unlimited AI usage, extra app themes, watermark-free exports, and more.

## Who it's for

Students first — the pricing, the bilingual EN/AR support, and the exam tool all point at a Palestinian/Arab-world student audience specifically, though nothing about the product is exclusive to that. The founder story (a teenager building the tool she wished existed) is a real, human differentiator worth keeping visible in branding — it's not a faceless startup.

## Personality and tone

Warm, encouraging, a little poetic — never corporate or clinical. Nuvora feels like a companion that's rooting for you, not a spreadsheet with a to-do list bolted on. Lumi's voice is the clearest expression of this: capable of a genuine compliment, capable of answering "who made you" with something closer to a line of poetry than a changelog entry.

## Visual identity

- **Color**: violet/lavender as the primary gradient (light purple → deep purple), with orange, pink, and blue as alternate Premium themes.
- **Mark**: a four-point sparkle (✦) is used as the current app icon/placeholder mark.
- **Type**: Outfit for display/headings, Inter for body text, JetBrains Mono for anything data-like.
- **Aesthetic**: soft glassmorphism — blurred, translucent panels, rounded corners, gradients, a genuinely good dark mode. It reads as calm and a little dreamy, closer to a night sky than a productivity dashboard.

## Why "Nuvora"

Nuvora was chosen as the rebrand from the original working title, "Aurora" — the earlier name carried the right emotional register (dawn, light appearing beautifully in the dark) but ran into naming-collision and domain-availability issues (AWS Aurora, existing games/products, `.com` unavailable). Nuvora keeps that same soft, glowing, "new beginning" quality while being distinct and ownable.

*Note: some internal, non-user-facing code (database values from before the rename, one-time localStorage migration keys, the original seeded demo account) still reference "aurora" on purpose — those exist purely so a user's existing account/browser data migrates cleanly to the new name rather than breaking. They're intentional compatibility scaffolding, not leftover branding.*

## Quick brief for a designer (copy-paste ready)

> App: Nuvora — a bilingual (EN/AR) student productivity app combining tasks, focus timers with a tree-planting gamification mechanic, an AI study companion, and an AI exam generator. Tone: warm, calm, a little poetic — not corporate. Existing palette: violet/lavender primary (#7C6AF0 → #5B47E0), with orange/pink/blue as alternates. Looking for a simple, flat logomark that works as a small app icon (readable at 16–32px) plus a wordmark in a rounded/friendly sans-serif (currently using Outfit).
