# Aurora — Brand & Naming Brief

*Prepared to help pick a name, logo direction, and domain.*

## What Aurora actually is

Aurora is an all-in-one "Life OS" for students — one app that replaces the five or six apps someone would otherwise juggle to organize their tasks, their studying, their focus time, and their headspace. It's built by Haneen Turkieh, a 19-year-old solo developer from Nablus, Palestine, and it's bilingual (English/Arabic) from the ground up.

The pitch in one line: **a calm, slightly poetic productivity app that turns discipline into something you actually want to do.**

## The pillars (what's actually inside)

- **Tasks & goals** — everyday to-dos, recurring tasks, milestones, a day planner.
- **Focus & the Forest** — a pomodoro-style focus timer where every completed session plants a tree in your personal Forest. Trees can die if you break focus. There are also shared "Rooms" — synced focus sessions with friends, so studying together isn't just a video call, it's a shared timer and a shared tree.
- **Habits & mood** — streak tracking plus daily mood logging.
- **Lumi** — an AI companion woven through the whole app. It remembers things across conversations, can log your mood or check your habit streaks for you, nudges you about deadlines, and has a warm, slightly poetic personality (ask it who built it and it answers like it means it).
- **Exam Assistant** — upload your notes or files, get an AI-generated practice exam (multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, mixed) with a real difficulty rubric and a real countdown timer. It's a personal exam simulator, not just a flashcard generator.
- **Launchpad** — a CV builder plus internship/project tracker, for the "what comes after graduation" part of student life.
- **Analytics & gamification** — XP, levels, streaks, growth charts. Discipline becomes visibly, satisfyingly a game.
- **Premium** — a subscription (monthly/semester/annual, priced in NIS) that unlocks unlimited AI usage, extra app themes, watermark-free exports, and more.

## Who it's for

Students first — the pricing, the bilingual EN/AR support, and the exam tool all point at a Palestinian/Arab-world student audience specifically, though nothing about the product is exclusive to that. The founder story (a teenager building the tool she wished existed) is a real, human differentiator worth keeping visible in branding — it's not a faceless startup.

## Personality and tone

Warm, encouraging, a little poetic — never corporate or clinical. Aurora feels like a companion that's rooting for you, not a spreadsheet with a to-do list bolted on. Lumi's voice is the clearest expression of this: capable of a genuine compliment, capable of answering "who made you" with something closer to a line of poetry than a changelog entry.

## Existing visual identity (what's already built)

- **Color**: violet/lavender as the primary gradient (light purple → deep purple), with orange, pink, and blue as alternate Premium themes.
- **Mark**: a four-point sparkle (✦) is already used as a placeholder app icon.
- **Type**: Outfit for display/headings, Inter for body text, JetBrains Mono for anything data-like.
- **Aesthetic**: soft glassmorphism — blurred, translucent panels, rounded corners, gradients, a genuinely good dark mode. It reads as calm and a little dreamy, closer to a night sky than a productivity dashboard.

## Why "Aurora" already works

Aurora carries two meanings that both map directly onto the product: **dawn** (a new beginning — literally what a fresh to-do list or a fresh study session is) and **aurora borealis** (light appearing beautifully in the dark — which is exactly the emotional note the app's dark-mode, cosmic aesthetic is already going for). It's evocative, it's already fully built into the codebase and every user-facing string, and it's not a name you have to explain.

The honest tradeoff: a full rename means touching a lot of surface area (every UI string, Lumi's own self-description, the legal pages, the whole visual system) for the sake of a fresher domain — worth doing only if a new name is genuinely stronger, not just different.

## Name directions

### Option A — Keep Aurora, solve it with a modifier
Lowest-risk, zero rebuild cost. The bare word is long gone everywhere, but a modifier reads clean:
- **auroralife.app** — available now, $9.99/yr. Directly states the "Life OS" positioning.

### Option B — Rename around "light + growth," the two things nobody else owns
Two features nobody else in the productivity-app space has: the tree-planting Forest mechanic, and an AI companion with an actual personality. A name that fuses "light" (dawn/glow) with "growth" (grove/forest) claims both at once.

- **Glowgrove** — available as **glowgrove.app**, $9.99/yr. "Glow" carries the Aurora/light identity forward; "grove" is a direct, literal nod to the Forest feature — arguably your single most memorable, non-generic mechanic. Reads as warm, a little whimsical, easy to say out loud, and immediately understandable even to someone who's never opened the app.
- **myflowbox.app** — also available, $9.99/yr, but weaker fit (doesn't reference light, growth, or the founder story) — a fallback, not a first choice.

**My honest recommendation:** if you want to keep momentum and avoid re-touching a fully built app, ship with **Aurora + auroralife.app**. If you want a name that's more distinctive and easier to trademark/own outright, **Glowgrove** is the strongest alternative I found — it's the only option that ties back to something Aurora actually does that competitors don't.

## Logo direction

Two concept sketches were generated alongside this brief (see the visual above in our conversation):

1. **Aurora mark** — three overlapping curved "ribbon" strokes in violet, pink, and blue (literally the northern-lights shape) with a small sparkle accent, inside a dark circular badge. Extends the existing ✦ mark into something more distinctive than a generic star.
2. **Glowgrove mark** — a simple tree silhouette backed by a soft radiating glow, in green and warm amber, inside the same dark circular badge treatment. Reads instantly as "light meets growth" without needing explanation.

Either direction should stay a **flat, simple silhouette that still reads clearly at 16px** (favicon size) — avoid anything with fine detail or more than 2–3 colors for the primary mark. A designer (or an AI image tool) can take either sketch and refine it into a production-ready icon set.
